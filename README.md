# Voryzhaekal

> The baker kneaded a cloud. Its rain remembered the shape of a hunger without a mouth.

## The Oven inside the White Hill

Voryzhaekal is found within a hill whose surface remains white throughout every season. Travelers mistake its covering for snow until they notice the small feathers growing where their footprints ought to be.

Inside the hill stands an oven of blue stone. It has no chimney. Its warmth rises through the feathers and gathers in a cloud that rests above the summit, unchanged by wind.

The baker works beside a table carved from dark horn. His bread is pale and weightless, with small hollows resembling closed eyes. Visitors may carry a loaf outside, but nobody has managed to remember its shape after returning home.

## The Hunger That Arrived without a Guest

One evening, a bowl appeared upon the table. It contained nothing visible, yet everyone in the room felt the presence of something waiting to be fed.

The baker placed a loaf beside it. The hollows in the bread opened, showing a tiny sky in which a long pale creature moved through rain. The bowl grew warm. The loaf remained untouched.

He kneaded another loaf, this time folding a feather into the dough. Before it entered the oven, a shadow spread across the blue stone, displaying wings wider than the hill.

From inside the bowl came a sound like someone inhaling beneath a closed door. The baker covered it with cloth. The feather disappeared from the dough, and a small white mark formed upon the cloud above the summit.

## The Apprentice's Recipe

The apprentice keeps a book of recipes without quantities. Each begins with a place, continues with a season, and ends with the name of someone who has never eaten at the table.

He attempted to bake the recipe of a distant orchard. The bread emerged carrying the scent of rain and the shadow of a tree. Its crust showed a small window facing a room where the apprentice himself was holding a recipe book.

He opened the loaf. The window vanished, leaving a narrow passage between its pale halves. At the passage's far end stood the empty bowl, much larger than it appeared upon the table.

The baker asked which season had been folded into the orchard. The apprentice could remember only a person waiting beside a closed door.

That recipe acquired one additional line. Nobody could read it while feeling hungry.

## The Creature above the Summit

Within the cloud, observers sometimes see a pale dragon whose wings appear to be made of the hill's feathers. Its body bends around a small blue opening that glows like the oven viewed from far away.

The creature does not fly. It remains still while the cloud moves through it, carrying fine rain that never reaches the hill. Each drop briefly reflects the bowl upon the baker's table.

A visitor tried collecting the rain. He placed a cup beneath the cloud and waited until his own shadow began growing feathers. Nothing fell into the cup.

Inside the hill, the baker found water in the bowl for the first time. Its surface showed the visitor holding an empty cup. Beneath that reflection moved a mouth with no visible body.

The baker added flour. The water disappeared, and the visitor's shadow returned to its familiar shape.

## The Language upon the Blue Stone

**Vor — yzhae — kal — iruun.**

These sounds appear along the oven's rim whenever the cloud becomes dark. Their letters resemble narrow streams of rain, though the stone remains dry.

The baker pronounces them while kneading a loaf that will never be placed inside. He folds the dough seven times, leaves it upon the horn table, and waits for its shadow to acquire a mouth.

An apprentice once spoke the sequence before the seventh fold. The oven's opening became transparent, revealing a white hill inside a small room. Above that hill, a cloud rested against a ceiling lined with feathers.

The baker finished the fold. The blue stone returned. The apprentice found a small horn-colored bowl inside his book, pressed flat between the unreadable line and the name of its absent guest.

## The Table's Unanswered Invitation

> Who waits inside the empty bowl?
>
> The hunger that has not chosen a voice.
>
> Where does the cloud leave its rain?
>
> In the room whose door remains folded into the loaf.
>
> Why does the baker keep one bread unbaked?
>
> Because someone above the hill is still remembering its mouth.

These replies were heard by visitors sitting around the horn table after the oven had cooled. The baker remained silent. His hands moved upon an empty cloth as though kneading something the others could not see.

By morning, the cloth contained a feather with a blue tip. Its shadow resembled the bowl, filled to the rim with a dark cloud.

## The Loaf That Remains upon the Table

The hill stays white. Its feathers grow where travelers leave footprints, and the cloud above its summit continues holding rain that cannot reach the ground.

The baker keeps the bowl covered. His apprentice preserves the recipe book without attempting to read its additional line. Sometimes the horn table warms beneath an empty place, as though a guest had rested a hand there.

Voryzhaekal waits inside the oven's silent heat. Above the summit, a pale creature folds its feathered wings around an opening of blue stone, listening for the hunger that has not yet learned to speak.
